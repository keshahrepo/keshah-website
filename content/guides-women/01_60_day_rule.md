## What stress does to your scalp

Notice something? When you're stressed, your jaw clenches. Your shoulders rise. Your forehead tightens.

Now feel your scalp.

It's tight too. You just never noticed because nobody told you to check.

When your body enters fight-or-flight, it tenses muscles everywhere. Including the frontalis, temporalis, and occipitalis — the muscles attached to your scalp. Chronic stress means chronic tension. Chronic tension restricts blood flow. Restricted blood flow starves follicles.

That's the actual mechanism.

The same thing happens with hormonal changes. Cortisol elevations, estrogen drops, thyroid shifts — they all increase the tension your scalp carries. The hormone is the trigger. The tension is what damages the follicle.

## The 60-day rule

Here's the actual protocol:

1. **Start the daily routine today.** Don't change anything else.
2. **Don't start HRT, thyroid medication, or hormone supplementation during this window.** If you're already on something, keep it. Don't introduce new variables.
3. **Don't add supplements, oils, or shampoo changes.** One variable at a time.
4. **Track your shedding.** Note how much hair you see in the shower drain or on your brush each week.
5. **At day 60, reassess.**

If your shedding has slowed or stopped, you've answered the question. You didn't need to fix your hormones. The protocol handled it.

If your shedding hasn't slowed at all by day 60, *then* it's time to escalate. Get a full bloodwork panel done. Talk to your doctor about thyroid, ferritin, vitamin D, and (if applicable) hormones. Your hair loss may be the rare case where the underlying cause needs direct treatment.

But for most women, you won't get there. The protocol will have already done the work.

## What to expect along the way

**Days 1–3**: Your scalp may feel a little sore after sessions. Some women feel tingling — that's blood circulation returning to areas it's been blocked from. Both are good signs.

**Days 3–7**: Your scalp will start to feel less tight. You'll be able to move it more easily under your fingers. This is the first real signal the protocol is working.

**Days 7–14**: You may notice *more* hair falling out than usual. **This is normal and a good sign.** When circulation returns to follicles, your scalp pushes out the weakest hairs — the ones that were dying anyway. Think of it like clearing dead leaves so new growth can come through.

**Days 30–60**: For most women, this is when the shedding noticeably slows. Hair fall reduces. Some start to see baby hairs at the hairline or part.

**Day 60**: Hair fall has generally stopped for most women. If it hasn't, that's your signal to dig deeper.

## Why women specifically

Women's hair loss is different from men's in one important way: it's usually more responsive to releasing scalp tension because women carry less long-term hormonal damage than men do. Men have decades of DHT-related miniaturization. Women's hair loss is more often driven by acute stressors — postpartum, perimenopause, a stressful life event, illness — which means the underlying follicle is more recoverable.

That's why the 60-day rule works so reliably for women. The follicles are still alive. They just need their blood supply back.
