# Guide Five — How to dermaroll without scarring your scalp

*A KESHAH publication*

---

Dermarolling works. There's real research behind it. Men on the protocol who add microneedling at the right depth, at the right frequency, often see noticeable regrowth in 3–6 months.

But it can also go badly. Dermarolling done wrong leaves permanent scars, causes folliculitis (scalp infection), and can accelerate hair loss instead of reversing it.

This guide is about how to do it safely. Most online advice gets one or more things wrong — depth, frequency, sterilization, post-care. Get any of those wrong long-term, and you're damaging your scalp.

**Important context:** The KESHAH scalp protocol works on its own. Most members stop their hair loss without ever picking up a dermaroller. If you're starting out, do the protocol first for 60 days. If you then want to add microneedling for regrowth, this guide is for you.

---

## What microneedling actually does

Dermarolling creates controlled micro-injuries in the scalp. These tiny injuries trigger two useful things:

1. **A healing response** — your body floods the area with blood, growth factors, and stem-cell-like repair activity.
2. **Better product penetration** — anything topical (minoxidil, peptides) absorbs deeper.

For pattern hair loss, it's the healing response that does the work. Increased local blood flow + growth factor activity can stimulate dormant follicles to re-enter the growth phase.

That's the mechanism. It's real and there's good research behind it — at the right depth, with the right frequency.

---

## How scarring actually happens

Microneedling scars in three ways:

1. **Going too deep too soon.** Pushing a 1.5mm roller through untrained scalp tissue tears the dermis instead of cleanly perforating it.
2. **Rolling over the same spot repeatedly.** Multiple passes in tight rotation creates overlapping micro-trauma. Your scalp can't heal what's constantly being re-injured.
3. **Using a dull or contaminated needle head.** Needles dull every 8–12 weeks of regular use. A dull needle drags through skin instead of puncturing it cleanly.

Avoid these three and you avoid scarring. Each is preventable.

---

## The depth question

Forum advice loves 1.5mm. "Go deep or go home." This is wrong for most men most of the time.

The correct approach is **progressive depth** — work up to 1.5mm over months, not days.

| Month | Depth | Purpose |
|---|---|---|
| Month 1 | 0.75 mm | Acclimate the scalp. Build tolerance. No bleeding. |
| Month 2 | 1.00 mm | Reaching deeper into the dermis. Light pinpoint bleeding is okay. |
| Month 3 | 1.25 mm | Approaching follicle depth. |
| Month 4+ | 1.50 mm | Maintenance / regrowth depth. |

**Why progressive depth matters:**

- 1.5mm on an untrained scalp = excessive damage and scarring risk
- 1.5mm on a built-up scalp = effective stimulation without harm
- Your scalp tissue thickens and becomes more resilient with progressive use — the same way calluses build

Most studies showing positive results used 0.6–1.0mm, not 1.5+. Depth is not "more is always better."

---

## Frequency: once a week, never more

This is where most men go wrong. They get the roller, see modest results, decide to do it every other day. That's the opposite of what helps.

**Right frequency: once per week.**

Why: the healing response takes 5–7 days to complete. Each session restarts the inflammation cycle. If you roll every 2–3 days, your scalp lives in chronic inflammation — which damages follicles faster than the rolling helps them.

Daily microneedling is a mistake. Even every-other-day is too much. Once a week is the maximum useful frequency for almost everyone.

---

## Sterilization (the rule that prevents infection)

Reusing an unsterilized roller is how you get folliculitis (scalp infection that requires antibiotics).

**Every single session:**
1. Soak the roller in 70% isopropyl alcohol for 10 minutes before use.
2. Rinse with distilled water after.
3. Soak again for 10 minutes after use.
4. Air dry in the open, head-down.
5. Store in the container it came in.

**Replace the roller every 8–12 weeks.** Old needles dull and tear instead of puncture. Cheap rollers dull faster than expensive ones.

Don't reuse needle heads across multiple months unless they're explicitly marketed as long-life.

## How to do a session safely

### Before

1. **Wash your hair the night before or that morning** with a gentle shampoo. Don't use product on the scalp.
2. **No alcohol for 24 hours before** — it thins blood and increases bruising risk.
3. **Sterilize the roller** in 70% isopropyl alcohol for 10 minutes.

### During

1. **Section your hair** so you can reach the scalp clearly.
2. **Roll in 4 directions over each section:** vertical, horizontal, diagonal one way, diagonal the other. 5–6 passes in each direction.
3. **Apply moderate pressure.** Enough that you feel the needles. Not enough to cause severe pain.
4. **Total session time: 10–15 minutes.** Not 30. Not 5.
5. **Cover all thinning areas evenly.** Don't over-roll any single spot.

### After

1. **No minoxidil for at least 4 hours.** Open micro-channels = increased absorption = potential heart side effects. Wait until the channels start to close.
2. **No styling products on the scalp for 24 hours.** Let it heal.
3. **No pools, hot tubs, saunas for 48 hours.** Open wounds + chlorine/bacteria = bad combo.
4. **Mild redness for 12–24 hours is normal.** Severe pain or bleeding past 30 minutes is not — stop and reassess depth.

---

## Red flags during/after a session

Stop and reassess if you see:

- **Bleeding that doesn't stop within 30 minutes** — too deep or too aggressive.
- **Severe pain during rolling** — depth is too high for your current tolerance.
- **Redness lasting more than 48 hours** — sign of inflammation, not normal healing.
- **Visible "tracks" or scratches** — the needle is dragging, not puncturing. Replace the roller.
- **Pimples or pus around hair follicles after a session** — folliculitis is starting. Stop sessions and see a doctor.

## Pen vs. roller

A note on equipment: there are two main tools — manual rollers ($15–$30) and motorized pens ($100–$300).

Pens offer better depth control and reduce skin-tearing. Worth the upgrade once you're committed.

For starting out, a quality manual roller is fine. Just buy a reputable brand (Stylo, ZGTS, Linduray, BeautyBio). Avoid cheap unbranded Amazon rollers — needle quality is inconsistent and you'll tear skin.

---

## Before you start

1. **Do the KESHAH protocol consistently for 60 days first.** Building scalp tissue and circulation before microneedling means the dermaroller works on healthier tissue.
2. **If you're on minoxidil, you're a good candidate.** The micro-channels improve absorption.
3. **If you have any active scalp conditions** (dermatitis, psoriasis, open wounds, recent transplant), wait. Treat the condition first.
4. **If you're on blood thinners or have a bleeding disorder, talk to your doctor first.**

---

## Bottom line

Microneedling works — but only when done right.

- Progressive depth: 0.75mm → 1.5mm over months.
- Once a week, never more.
- Sterilize before and after. Replace every 8–12 weeks.
- 4-hour delay before any topicals.
- Foundation first: KESHAH protocol for 60 days before adding microneedling.

Done correctly, it's a powerful addition. Done wrong, it scars or accelerates hair loss.
