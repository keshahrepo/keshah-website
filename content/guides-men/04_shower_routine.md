# Guide Four — 3 minute shower routine to fix dandruff or oily scalp

*A KESHAH publication*

You don't need any of that.

The actual fix is a $10 silicone scalp massager from Amazon and 3 minutes of your shower time. That's it. I'll walk you through exactly what to do — and why most "dandruff treatments" make the problem worse.

---

## What's actually causing the problem

### Dandruff

That flaky white stuff on your shoulders isn't usually "dry scalp" (which is what shampoo brands want you to think — so they can sell you more product).

Real dandruff is usually one of three things:

1. **Trapped sebum** — your scalp's natural oil that's gotten stuck in the follicles and oxidized. Looks like waxy flakes.
2. **Fungal overgrowth** — a yeast called *Malassezia* that lives on everyone's scalp but overgrows in certain conditions. Causes itching and inflammation along with flaking.
3. **Over-washing irritation** — when you shampoo too often, the constant stripping irritates the scalp into shedding skin cells more rapidly. The "dandruff shampoo" then makes it worse by stripping more.

### Oily scalp

Your scalp produces oil to protect itself. When you shampoo daily, you strip that oil. Your scalp panics, overproduces oil to compensate, and you wake up greasy again 24 hours later. The more you wash, the worse it gets.

This is why people who switch from washing daily to washing every 3–4 days *almost always* end up with less oily hair after a 3–4 week transition period.

## The 3-minute shower routine

Here's the entire protocol. Do this every shower — whether you're shampooing or not.

### What you need

**A silicone scalp massager.** $5–$15 on Amazon. Search "silicone scalp massager shower."

The brand doesn't matter. Most of them are the same product. Look for one with soft silicone bristles (not stiff plastic) and a comfortable handle.

### The technique

1. **In the shower, wet your scalp thoroughly.** Hot water opens pores and softens trapped sebum.
2. **Apply your shampoo OR just keep your scalp wet.** Either works. The massager does the lifting either way.
3. **Scrub your entire scalp for 3–4 minutes** using the silicone massager.
4. **Use firm, circular motions.** Not gentle. Not aggressive. Firm.
5. **Cover every section of the scalp** — top, sides, back, and especially the nape of the neck where buildup is worst.
6. **Rinse thoroughly.**

That's it. 3–4 minutes. Once or twice a week if you're keeping shampoo. Every shower if you're going shampoo-free.

### Why this works

The silicone massager mechanically does three things at once:

- **Exfoliates** dead skin cells from your scalp (the actual cause of most flaking).
- **Releases trapped sebum** from clogged follicles (the cause of waxy flakes and oily roots).
- **Increases circulation** to the follicles (which compounds with your KESHAH protocol).

You don't need anti-fungal medicated shampoos for most dandruff. You just need the buildup gone — and that's a mechanical problem, not a chemical one.

## What to expect

### If you have dandruff

For most men, dandruff visibly reduces within 2 weeks of using the massager every shower. Most cases disappear entirely within 30 days. The scalp recalibrates as the buildup clears and circulation improves.

### If you have an oily scalp

Your scalp needs 3–4 weeks to adjust if you've been over-washing. Here's the path:

- **Week 1:** Wash every other day instead of daily. Use the silicone massager every shower (even on no-shampoo days, just with water).
- **Week 2:** Wash every 2–3 days. Continue the scalp massager every shower.
- **Week 3:** Wash twice a week.
- **Week 4 onward:** Wash once or twice a week as needed.

By week 4, your scalp produces less oil because it's no longer in panic mode. Your hair will feel fresher between washes, not worse.

### If you have both (flaky AND oily)

This is common and treatable with the same routine. The silicone massager handles both problems simultaneously — exfoliating the flakes while releasing trapped sebum.

## What NOT to do

Avoid these — they make things worse, not better:

- **Don't use sulfate-heavy "clarifying" shampoos.** They strip your scalp more aggressively, restarting the oil cycle.
- **Don't shampoo daily** to combat oily hair. It's the cause, not the cure.
- **Don't scrub with your fingernails.** You'll cause micro-tears and irritation. Use the silicone tool.
- **Don't use coconut oil or hair oils on your scalp.** They clog follicles and trap more bacteria/fungus underneath.
- **Don't use dandruff shampoos with harsh active ingredients (selenium sulfide, coal tar, zinc pyrithione) long-term.** They work short-term but degrade scalp barrier over time. They're for emergencies, not daily use.

## Seborrheic dermatitis (when this isn't enough)

If you have red, raised, persistent flaky patches that itch intensely — especially around your hairline, eyebrows, or behind your ears — you may have *seborrheic dermatitis* (a more inflammatory version of dandruff).

For this:
- Keep doing the 3-minute routine but use lighter pressure on the inflamed areas.
- Add a 2% ketoconazole shampoo (Nizoral) once a week, but no more — only for the flare-ups.
- If it's not improving within 4–6 weeks, see a dermatologist. They can prescribe stronger short-term treatment if needed.

---

## When to see a doctor

Almost no one needs to. But see one if:

- The flaking is bleeding or oozing.
- The itching is severe and disrupting sleep.
- You see sudden, large, patchy hair loss in the same areas (could be alopecia areata, not dandruff-related).
- The 3-minute routine hasn't improved things at all after 6 weeks.
