# Guide Three — Ingredients that damage scalp health (check your shampoo)

*A KESHAH publication*

---

I want to start with something that might sound radical:

**I don't think we need shampoo.**

I don't shampoo at all anymore. No conditioner either. I clean with water and a silicone scalp massager. That's it.

This isn't a hot take. Your scalp produces its own oils — *sebum* — to keep itself and your hair healthy. Shampoo strips those oils, your scalp panics and produces more oil to compensate, so you wash again. It's a cycle the entire haircare industry depends on you not noticing.

Most men don't actually need shampoo. We've just been trained that we do.

But I know not everyone is ready to go cold turkey. So this guide does two things:

1. Shows you the ingredients to actually avoid in your current shampoo.
2. Walks you through how to phase off shampoo entirely if you want to try it.

---

## The shampoo cycle (and why your scalp is oily 24 hours after washing)

Here's what's happening when you wash your hair:

1. Shampoo (specifically, **sulfates**) strips your scalp's natural sebum.
2. Your scalp senses the oil is gone and panics — it thinks your skin barrier is compromised.
3. It overproduces sebum to compensate. You wake up the next morning with oily roots.
4. You wash again. The cycle restarts. Worse than yesterday.

This is why shampoo brands love marketing "oily hair" solutions to you. The shampoo itself is what makes your hair oily. Then they sell you the fix.

The cycle is solvable. Your scalp will normalize oil production within 3–4 weeks of washing less often. But you have to push through the awkward transition.

## Ingredients to actually avoid

These are the ones that matter — not the marketing-friendly "free from parabens!" labels.

### 1. Sulfates (SLS, SLES)

What they are: harsh detergents that create the foamy lather you associate with "clean."
Why they're bad: they strip sebum aggressively, which triggers the oil-overproduction cycle. They can also irritate the scalp directly.

**Look for:** "Sodium Lauryl Sulfate," "Sodium Laureth Sulfate," "Ammonium Lauryl Sulfate"
**Buy:** "Sulfate-free" shampoos. Almost every major brand has a sulfate-free line now.

### 2. Silicones (especially non-water-soluble ones)

What they are: synthetic ingredients that coat the hair shaft to make it feel smooth.
Why they're bad: they build up on the scalp over time, clogging follicles and reducing blood flow. They also coat your hair so other products and water can't penetrate.

**Look for:** "Dimethicone," "Cyclomethicone," "Amodimethicone" (anything ending in "-cone" or "-conol")
**Note:** Water-soluble silicones (PEG-modified) are less problematic but still avoid if possible.

### 3. Synthetic fragrance

What it is: an unregulated catch-all that can contain hundreds of undisclosed chemicals.
Why it's bad: a leading cause of scalp irritation, contact dermatitis, and inflammation. Inflammation = restricted blood flow = follicle damage.

**Look for:** "Fragrance," "Parfum," "Aroma"
**Buy:** Products with essential oils for scent (e.g., "rosemary oil," "peppermint oil") or completely unscented.

### 4. Parabens

What they are: preservatives (Methylparaben, Propylparaben, Butylparaben).
Why they're contested: some research suggests they're hormone disruptors that can mimic estrogen. The evidence isn't conclusive, but if you're concerned about your hormonal balance, why add a maybe-disruptor to your scalp twice a week?

**Look for:** anything ending in "-paraben"
**Buy:** "Paraben-free."

### 5. Drying alcohols

What they are: short-chain alcohols (Alcohol Denat., Isopropyl Alcohol, Propanol).
Why they're bad: they dehydrate the hair and scalp, breaking down the moisture barrier.
**Note:** "Fatty alcohols" like Cetyl Alcohol and Cetearyl Alcohol are different — they're actually moisturizing. The bad ones are the short-chain ones.

### 6. Formaldehyde-releasing preservatives

What they are: preservatives that slowly release formaldehyde.
Why they're bad: formaldehyde is a known irritant and carcinogen. It's been linked to hair loss.

**Look for:** "DMDM Hydantoin," "Quaternium-15," "Imidazolidinyl Urea," "Diazolidinyl Urea"
**Buy:** Anything without these.

## How to read a label in 10 seconds

You don't need to memorize chemistry. Use this rule:

**The first 5 ingredients on the bottle are 80%+ of what's in it.** If any of the first 5 are sulfates, silicones, or fragrance, put it back.

You can also use the "Yuka" app or "Think Dirty" app — scan the barcode and get an instant rating. Free, fast, takes the guesswork out.

## If you want to phase off shampoo entirely

Don't quit cold turkey. Your scalp will be an oil slick for weeks and you'll hate it.

Phase off over 5 weeks:

- **Week 1:** Shampoo every other day (instead of daily).
- **Week 2:** Shampoo every 2–3 days.
- **Week 3:** Shampoo twice a week.
- **Week 4:** Shampoo once a week.
- **Week 5+:** Try water-only washing with a silicone scalp massager.

During the transition, your hair *will* feel oilier. This is your scalp readjusting after years of being stripped. By week 4–5, oil production normalizes. Your hair will actually feel cleaner and look healthier than it did when you were washing daily.

When you do shampoo, use a clean, sulfate-free, silicone-free, fragrance-free option. There are many — Just Natural, Acure, 100% Pure, Innersense, Briogeo are options. None of them are essential brands. Find one with clean ingredients and an acceptable price.

## The one rule for everything else

Whatever shampoo you use, whatever styling products you use, follow this rule:

**Keep products off your scalp.**

Use shampoo on your roots only when needed, lengths get the conditioner. Apply styling products (mousse, leave-in, serum) only on the hair lengths, not the scalp. Avoid dry shampoo and texturizing powders — they sit directly on follicles and choke them off.

If anything lands on your scalp accidentally, rinse it out the next time you shower with a silicone scalp massager (3–4 minutes of circular scrubbing).

That single rule does more for scalp health than any "clean ingredient" shampoo.

---

## Bottom line

Your shampoo probably isn't the main reason you're losing hair. But it's not helping, and the wrong ingredients can absolutely make scalp inflammation worse — which compounds the tension issue we're addressing with the protocol.

Check your current bottle against the 6 ingredients above. If any of the worst offenders are in the top 5 ingredients, swap to something cleaner. If you want to go further, phase off shampoo entirely over 5 weeks.

Whatever you choose: keep products off your scalp. That's the rule that actually matters.
